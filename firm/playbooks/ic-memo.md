# Playbook: IC memo

How to produce a Sunshine Equity **Investment Committee memo**—the primary write-up used to approve a new investment or a material follow-on / confirmatory decision.

## Purpose

- Give IC a decision-ready view of the company, deal, thesis, risks, and returns.
- Create the lasting record of *why* we invested (or passed), used later in LP letters, portfolio reviews, and follow-ons.
- Audience: Investment Committee and deal team—not a marketing deck.

## When to use

- New platform or growth investment seeking IC approval
- Confirmatory IC before close (update since prior approval)
- Material follow-on or structure change that requires IC

For LP-facing capital call language after approval, use a separate capital-call playbook when one exists; the IC memo is the internal decision doc.

## Inputs (gather before drafting)

- Company overview materials (management deck, data room excerpts)
- Deal terms sheet / LOI / model (entry valuation, structure, ownership, board)
- Financial historicals and projections (with source and as-of dates)
- Diligence status and advisor list (accounting, legal, commercial, tech)
- Reference-call themes (even if full notes stay offline)
- Comps and market context
- Prior IC notes or deal-meeting updates, if any
- Strong examples in neutron under `documents/investment-committee-materials/` (search by company name)

## Outline (standard sections)

Match the firm’s historical memo shape (see neutron IC examples). Use clear headings; keep tables for financials and returns.

1. **Header** — Company name, date, author/deal team, fund vehicle(s), decision requested (e.g. approve up to $X investment).
2. **Company overview** — What they do, products, customers, revenue model, geography, founding/ownership snapshot, leadership.
3. **Financials** — Historical revenue/EBITDA (or relevant metrics), growth rates, margins; forecast cases with explicit assumptions; unit economics if core to thesis.
4. **Deal terms** — Check size, primary vs secondary, security (e.g. preferred), liquidation preference, dividends, valuation (pre/post, entry multiples), ownership, board seats, milestones/drawdowns, ratchets or other protections.
5. **Process / sourcing** — How the deal was sourced (thematic sprint, relationship, bank process), timeline, competitive dynamics.
6. **Diligence** — Workstreams completed/in flight; advisors; key findings (can be summary with “open items”).
7. **Investment thesis** — Numbered, evidence-backed reasons we win (market, product, unit economics, team, entry price, structure). Tie each point to data.
8. **Returns analysis** — Base / upside / downside (or firm-standard cases): MOIC, IRR, exit year, exit multiple, key drivers and sensitivities.
9. **Risks and mitigants** — Honest risks paired with what we believe mitigates them (or residual exposure).
10. **Adjacencies / upside options** (if material) — Product, geo, M&A, cross-sell not in base case.
11. **Appendix** (optional) — Detail tables, org chart, product screens, full comps.

## Quality bar

- **Decision-first:** A partner should know the ask and the recommendation within the first page.
- **Sourced numbers:** Every material figure has an as-of date and source (management, audit, model version).
- **Thesis ≠ description:** “What the company does” is not the thesis; thesis is why *this* deal at *this* price/structure works for Sunshine.
- **Structure clarity:** Preferred terms, preference, and governance are explicit—not buried.
- **Risks are real:** Include what could break the base case; avoid boilerplate only.
- **Consistent cases:** Returns cases use the same metric definitions as the model; label gross vs net if both appear.
- **Firm language:** Use [GLOSSARY.md](../GLOSSARY.md) (sourcing, preferred, MOIC, etc.).

## Common failure modes

- Leading with market poetry and burying deal terms
- Forecasts without drivers or without a downside
- Missing primary/secondary split or fund vehicle
- Thesis points that are not falsifiable or not tied to diligence
- Copying management claims without labeling them as such
