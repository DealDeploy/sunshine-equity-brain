# Glossary

Firm language used at Sunshine Equity. Prefer these meanings when writing or interpreting work product.

## Deal flow & process

**Sourcing** — Deal sourcing: finding new companies to potentially invest in. Includes outbound (thematic outreach, sprints), inbound (bankers, management intros, network), and relationship-driven pipeline. Distinct from diligence (evaluating a live opportunity once engaged).

**Sprint** — A focused workstream to explore a new market (or sub-sector) for companies to potentially source or invest in. Typical outputs: market map, target list, outreach plan, and a short read on whether the theme is investable for Sunshine. A sprint is *exploratory*; it is not itself an investment process on a single company.

**Pipeline** — The set of live and prospective opportunities under discussion, usually staged (e.g. early look → active diligence → IC → signed → closed).

**IC (Investment Committee)** — The body that approves (or declines) investments and material follow-ons. IC materials (especially the **IC memo**) are the primary decision record for a deal.

**Diligence** — Work to validate thesis, risks, financials, legal/structure, and references after a company is in active process. Often supported by external advisors (accounting, legal, commercial, tech).

**Confirmatory** — Late-stage diligence after commercial alignment (often post-LOI), focused on closing conditions, final terms, and residual risks before funding.

**LOI** — Letter of intent: non-binding (usually) outline of proposed deal terms before definitive docs.

## Capital structure & returns

**Preferred equity (preferred)** — Equity senior to common in liquidation and often with negotiated protections (liquidation preference, dividends, board rights, ratchets). Sunshine historically structures many growth investments as preferred.

**Liquidation preference** — Order and multiple of capital returned to preferred holders before common in a sale or liquidation (e.g. 1.0x non-participating).

**MOIC** — Multiple on invested capital: total value (realized + unrealized) divided by capital invested. Gross MOIC is before fees/carry; net is after.

**IRR** — Internal rate of return on cash flows over time. Gross vs net as with MOIC.

**Primary vs secondary** — Primary capital goes onto the company balance sheet (growth); secondary buys shares from existing holders (liquidity). Many Sunshine deals mix both.

**Follow-on** — Additional capital into an existing portfolio company after the initial investment.

**Exit** — Realization of an investment (sale, recap, IPO, etc.).

**Hold period** — Time from investment to exit (or to valuation date when discussing unrealized marks).

## Funds & portfolio

**Fund I / II** — Successive Sunshine Equity Partners fund vintages.

**LP** — Limited partner: an investor in a Sunshine fund.

**GP** — General partner: the Sunshine entity managing the fund.

**Portfolio company (portco)** — A company in which a Sunshine fund has invested.

**Mark / valuation** — Current carrying value of an unrealized investment, typically updated on a quarterly cadence per firm policy and GAAP.

## Firm work product

**IC memo** — Investment committee memorandum: the core write-up seeking approval of a new investment or material change. See [playbooks/ic-memo.md](./playbooks/ic-memo.md).

**Capital call letter / notice** — LP communication calling capital for a new investment, follow-on, and/or fees/expenses; often includes a short thesis and deal summary.

**Board materials** — Decks, narratives, and minutes for portfolio company board meetings (monthly/quarterly depending on company).

**Portfolio review** — Internal (and sometimes LP-facing) periodic review of portfolio company performance, terms, and outlook.

**Retention analysis** — Analysis of customer/revenue retention (and related cohorts, churn, NRR/GRR where applicable) used in diligence or portfolio monitoring.

## Market & research

**Theme / thematic** — A sector or sub-sector thesis that guides sourcing (e.g. solar asset software, industrial heat electrification). Sprints often validate or refine themes.

**TAM / SAM / SOM** — Total / serviceable / obtainable market sizing language used in memos; treat as estimates unless third-party verified.

**Comps** — Comparable public companies or transactions used for valuation and positioning.

When a term is ambiguous in a draft, define it once in-context or point here. Prefer firm usage over generic PE jargon when they differ.
