# Research: Industrial heat electrification

**Status:** Starter research note (non-canonical). Update as sprints and diligence produce better evidence.
**Related firm context:** Sunshine has invested across process-heat software and equipment-adjacent marketplaces (Kilnwise, Amberline—verify in `neutron/`).

## Why this theme matters for Sunshine

Industrial process heat is a large, slow-moving spend category. Software that helps plants plan, specify, and operate electrified heat (heat pumps, e-boilers, hybrid systems) sits next to hardware cycles that last a decade. Recurring software and marketplace take-rate models can attach to that spend without owning the equipment.

## Trend map (working)

### 1. Mid-temperature heat is becoming software-shaped

- Plants below ~200°C are the first wave for heat-pump retrofits.
- Specification, utility-incentive paperwork, and measurement & verification create a workflow layer that is still spreadsheet-heavy.

### 2. Capex decision support vs. runtime optimization

- Kilnwise-style runtime software (setpoints, load shifting, anomaly detection) is a different buyer than Amberline-style procurement marketplaces.
- Cross-sell is plausible but not automatic; treat as adjacency, not base case.

### 3. Utility incentives and reporting

- Incentive programs change annually by region. Software that stays current on incentive rules has a switching-cost story.
- Validate per geography; do not nationalize one state’s program into TAM.

### 4. Hardware OEMs as channel or competitor

- OEMs may bundle monitoring; independent software can still win on multi-vendor fleets.
- Channel interviews should separate “will they partner” from “will they build.”
